1. Direction and evidence boundaries

My primary target is Technical AI Consultant, with Digital Transformation Consultant as a complementary route into energy-sector engagements. My strongest positioning combines energy-sector experience, project management and practical AI prototyping. I want clients to understand what a system can do, what its evidence supports and where a person must make the final decision.

This is a completed planning draft, not a record of completed future activities. First-person reflections are proposed wording based on my shared background and should be reviewed for personal accuracy. No revenue, accuracy, time saving, certification award or employment outcome is claimed.

The supplied lab is optional and gives no numerical grading rubric. It requires GitHub submission only if the work is shared for review, with Markdown or PDF notes in a repository dedicated to this lab. This package covers Kick-off, all five CFU checkpoints, Core Tasks 1-4, Reinforce and Stretch. Decision rules

- Prioritize energy consultancies, utilities, infrastructure teams and public-sector transformation work where my domain experience is useful.

- Use Germany and EU-based opportunities first; treat international postings as skills evidence unless language, location and work conditions fit.

- Keep one main portfolio story: EU Tender Intelligence. Use Grid Connection Pre-Check as a second, explicitly limited prototype story.

- Prefer a small reproducible demonstration and a clear limitations statement over unsupported claims of production readiness.

2. Kick-off reflection and role targeting

Question 1: Which weeks were most energizing?

Weeks 3, 4 and 6 are my proposed choices. In Week 3, retrieval and tool integration gave me a way to connect language models with energy-sector information. Week 4 made the work tangible through LangGraph and n8n: separate steps could become a repeatable workflow. Week 6 added the questions I would face as a consultant, including whether an output is reliable, how it should be evaluated and what risks need explaining to a client. The attraction was the combination of a working system and a defensible recommendation.

Question 2: What work would I choose daily?

Advising clients is my first preference, supported by building and testing prototypes. I also value managing delivery when it helps a team move from a business question to a useful result. A proposed working mix is 40% discovery and advice, 35% prototyping and evaluation, and 25% delivery coordination and communication. These percentages are a planning preference, not measured time use.

Question 3: What would I avoid?

I would avoid roles dominated by isolated coding with little client or sector context, or administrative reporting without influence over decisions. I do not want to sell an AI system on the strength of a polished demonstration alone. I am willing to debug integrations and document delivery because those activities make my advice more credible.

4. Task 2: Portfolio narratives

These are proposed first-person case-study summaries based on work already described. Confirm each sentence against the final project files before using it in an application. Outcome statements describe an artifact or demonstrated approach, not validated client benefits.

Project 1: EU Tender Intelligence

Energy consultancies need to decide which funding opportunities deserve attention before spending time on proposals. I developed an EU Tender Intelligence prototype that connects opportunity intake with structured assessment and a bid/no-bid decision workflow. The project brings together Python, n8n and an agent-based design so that information gathering, assessment and reporting can be explained as separate steps. It produced a demonstrable approach to organizing decision evidence and made integration, credential and dataflow failures visible during development. The next validation step is to compare its recommendations and processing time with a documented human baseline; no measured productivity improvement is claimed yet.

Project 2: Grid Connection Pre-Check

Early grid-connection discussions need a clear view of what can be screened before a detailed engineering study. I developed a Grid Connection Pre-Check proof of concept using pandapower calculations and a consulting presentation that separates engineering analysis, AI explanation and human approval. The scope focused on communicating a preliminary screening result and its limitations. The output demonstrates how technical calculations can support an understandable client conversation without presenting the prototype as a formal connection decision. Independent engineering review and a benchmark against a validated case remain necessary before operational use.

Responsible-AI story

In the tender project, a persuasive summary can conceal missing information or uncertain interpretation. My proposed portfolio story explains why source references, explicit unknowns and human review matter before a bid decision. The next demonstration should include a missing-source case that is escalated instead of silently treated as reliable. I will also document which input fields are needed, remove unnecessary personal information from shared examples, and record model calls and token use when comparing workflow options. These are planned controls and validation evidence, not a claim that all safeguards already operate or that legal compliance has been certified.

Proposed evaluation record

For every test, log date, commit, input identifier, expected behaviour, observed behaviour, pass/fail, source references, latency, model/token cost where available and corrective action. For the 20-question set, calculate supported-answer rate as supported answers divided by all questions; report abstentions separately. Do not let a high average conceal a missing mandatory condition. For business value, compare median manual and assisted review time on the same small sample, including human correction time. Leave all performance fields unfilled until runs exist.
